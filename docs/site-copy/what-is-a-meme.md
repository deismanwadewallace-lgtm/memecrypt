# An idea with a body designed for circulation.

A meme is not necessarily an image. It is a reproducible cultural pattern: an
idea, attitude, reaction, or instruction that has acquired a body capable of
travelling.

That body may be a photograph, drawing, phrase, sound, dance, gesture,
misspelling, grammatical pattern, or ritual. Images dominate the internet
because they compress context quickly, but the image is often only the meme’s
temporary host.

When the body changes and the idea remains recognizable, the meme has mutated.

## Commentary

A meme reacts to politics, culture, work, identity, or ordinary life. It does
not arrive neutrally. Even the smallest reaction image carries a point of view.

## Compression

A meme reduces a complicated situation to a form that can be recognized before
it is fully examined. That efficiency is its brilliance and its danger.

## Positioning

A meme says more than “here is an idea.” It also says: “this is how people like
us feel about it.” Recognition becomes affiliation.

## Transmission

A meme invites repetition, alteration, and circulation. Its survival depends
on new hosts knowing enough to reproduce it, but not so much that the form
cannot change.

---

> A meme is an idea with a body designed for circulation.

> A meme does not merely encapsulate an idea. It can replace the work of
> examining one.

This is the tension at the centre of Memeography: memes as brilliant cultural
shorthand, and memes as substitutes for thought.

The image is not the meme. It is evidence of where the meme lived for a while.
