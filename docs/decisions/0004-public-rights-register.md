# ADR 0004: The rights register stays public

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-23

## Context

The repository is public and the rights register is linked directly from
`/museum/other-exhibits`. The register contains enforcement histories,
evidence grades, and the museum's assessment of which specimens carry risk.
After the 23 August update, 23 of 29 rows are graded below `Verified`.

This may or may not have been a deliberate choice. It requires one either way.

## Decision

The register stays public, with three conditions.

1. **Every row carries an evidence grade.** A public document that does not
   distinguish a court record from a recollection is worse than no document.
2. **No row asserts a legal conclusion.** The register records what happened
   and what the museum will do. It does not state whether a party would prevail.
3. **The standing disclaimer stays at the bottom of the Register sheet.** This
   is a curatorial working document, not legal advice, and rows graded below
   `Verified` require counsel before their specimen is displayed.

## Rationale

Publishing the constraint is the strongest position available. A museum that
shows its rights reasoning is transparent about its method instead of merely
claiming good practice. The register also converts the empty wing from a
mystery into an argument: the visitor can see why each object is absent.

The alternative, a private register behind a public wing, produces a museum
that says objects are missing and declines to say why. That is the posture the
project exists to criticise.

## Consequences

The museum's uncertainty is public. Twenty-three rows say the research is not
fully verified, and anyone can read that. This is acceptable and useful: it is
an open invitation to correct the record, and it sets the standard other rows
are held to.

Rights holders can also read their own entries. That is a feature. An entry
written so that its subject could read it without objection is an entry written
correctly.
