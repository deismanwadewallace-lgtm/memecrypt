# ADR 0006: Build Orthographic Mutations as an original-text collection

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-23

## Decision

Create the **Department of Orthographic Mutations** as a provisional Memeography
research collection at `/museum/orthographic-mutations`.

The collection studies twelve forms in which language itself became a meme:
punctuation rescue, Oxford-comma allegiance, word-boundary error, reflexive
correction failure, transposed-letter reading, the -ough cluster, intentional
`teh`, Shift-key leakage, missing-verb syntax, LOLspeak, Dogespeak, and
alternating-capital sarcasm.

Each object is rendered as original typography, diagram, or interface-like
notation. The page does not reproduce circulating meme images, screenshots,
logos, identifiable people, famous character art, or source captions.

## Registration boundary

The objects receive provisional identifiers `OM-01` through `OM-12`. These are
not permanent register IDs. The `M##` register remains at M01–M29 until a
separate import establishes sufficient provenance, rights analysis, and
curatorial intent for any new permanent specimen.

Where the linguistic form relates to an existing registered visual meme, the
collection links the records without changing their display decisions:

- LOLspeak → M28 (LOLCats), D3 / unavailable.
- Dogespeak → M01 (Doge), D2 / declined.

## Source posture

Research links document that a form circulated and help establish its history.
They are not evidence that a particular image is free to reproduce. Entries
with disputed or simplified origin stories say so in their label copy; most
notably, the “Cambridge research” attribution attached to typoglycemia is
treated as part of the specimen’s misinformation history, not as a fact.

## Writers at Work annex

Add a smaller interpretive annex built from original museum copy about
recurring writing-meme motifs. These field notes do not claim that one canonical
image or exact phrasing owns “writer’s block,” deadline avoidance, research
rabbit holes, revision, or characters departing from an outline.

## Consequences

- The museum can present a visually rich language exhibit without weakening
  ADR 0003 or the rights-register rendering rule.
- Famous visual hosts—the Alot creature, cat photographs, Kabosu, and Mocking
  SpongeBob—remain research context, not display material.
- The collection is usable now and upgradeable later: a future permanent
  registration can cite an `OM-##` record rather than beginning from scratch.
