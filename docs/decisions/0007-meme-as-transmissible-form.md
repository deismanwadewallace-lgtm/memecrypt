# ADR 0007: Define a meme as a transmissible form, not necessarily an image

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-23

## Decision

Memeography defines a meme as **an idea with a body designed for circulation**.
The body may be a photograph, drawing, phrase, sound, dance, gesture,
misspelling, grammatical pattern, or ritual. The image is a common and highly
efficient host, but it is not the essence of the meme.

When the body changes while the idea remains recognizable, the meme has
mutated.

The museum interprets memes through four functions:

1. **Commentary** — reacting to politics, culture, work, identity, or life.
2. **Compression** — reducing a complicated situation to a quickly recognized
   form.
3. **Positioning** — signalling how a group feels and who belongs to it.
4. **Transmission** — inviting repetition, alteration, and circulation.

## Critical tension

Memeography holds two claims together:

> A meme is an idea with a body designed for circulation.

> A meme does not merely encapsulate an idea. It can replace the work of
> examining one.

The museum will neither dismiss memes as trivial pictures nor treat compression
as understanding. Their usefulness as cultural shorthand and their capacity to
substitute feeling for inquiry are part of the same phenomenon.

## Consequences

- Acquisitions and exhibits may treat phrases, audio, gestures, syntax, and
  rituals as specimens when their repeatable form and cultural transmission are
  documented.
- Removing a copyrighted image does not necessarily remove the meme. An exhibit
  can isolate the surviving linguistic or behavioural mechanism.
- Visual analysis remains central, but the museum must distinguish a meme from
  its most famous visual host.
- The Department of Orthographic Mutations is the first collection built
  explicitly under this definition.
